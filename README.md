## Hi, I'm Giancarlo 👋

I am a **Senior PMO and IT Project Management professional** with **9+ years of experience** working across construction, banking, telecommunications, energy and public infrastructure delivered through Peru's **Works for Taxes (OxI)** mechanism.

Project management comes naturally to me. At least, that is what the people who keep assigning me projects say 😄. I enjoy bringing order to complex environments, aligning teams, controlling budgets and service levels, and translating operational data into decisions. It also pays the bills, or so the theory goes.

What genuinely keeps me curious, however, is **building something that works and solves a real problem**. That is why I combine project governance with executive dashboards, data preparation and practical automation. If a repetitive task can be made faster, safer and more traceable, I will probably try to understand it and improve it.

> **A clean history gives you traceability. A messy one turns documentation into archaeology: you have to dig through commits, files and records to find out what happened.**

## Why a PMO is learning Git and GitHub

In large organizations, information can easily disappear into a maze of files, versions, emails and approvals. This affects not only code, but also management information: reports, evidence, agreements, decisions, figures and responsibilities.

I do not want every improvement to depend entirely on the availability of a developer temporarily assigned from another team. Client priorities understandably change, and promising ideas can stop before a working design exists. So I decided to return to programming and automation, supported by AI, while remaining responsible for the requirements, logic, testing and final result.

Git gives me the control layer that this way of working needs:

- **Traceability:** understand what changed, why it changed and who validated it.
- **Review:** compare AI-assisted proposals before accepting them.
- **Recovery:** return to a stable version when an experiment becomes too creative.
- **Documentation:** preserve the reasoning behind technical and management decisions.
- **Contribution:** share useful, anonymized solutions and learn in public.

GitHub is becoming both my **portfolio of dashboards and automations** and the visible record of my development journey.

### From control points to automated SLA simulation

**Design non-invasive controls** → **Track with HERA** → **Analyze insights** → **Refine and expand controls** → **Automate with PERSEO**

1. **Initial design and implementation:** internal TCS control points were introduced in the Development Factory, specifically across the **Analysis and Construction** phases. They were designed to fit into the existing workflow without reducing developer productivity or affecting project delivery.
2. **Measurement with HERA:** I built HERA in Excel/VBA as the follow-up tool. Its forms supported planning, evaluation, charting and reporting; it validated information submitted by project managers, managed approved exceptions, calculated indicators and generated weekly rankings, diagnoses, impacts and recommendations.
3. **Learning from evidence:** HERA made recurring patterns, bottlenecks and SLA risks visible. Those insights were used to redefine the original control points and expand the model beyond Development into **QA/Certification** and **Production Deployment**, creating an End-to-End perspective.
4. **Evolution into PERSEO:** HERA's insights and control logic were combined with **HERCULES**, the existing solution associated with Application Maintenance (AM/post-production). This evolution produced **PERSEO**, an online and centralized platform used internally by TCS to keep simulated SLA results current.

My contribution focused on the **functional design, process model, approval criteria, exception rules, indicator definitions and mathematical SLA logic**. PERSEO replaced the recurring manual SLA simulation with centralized, updated results, allowing TCS teams to detect risks and implement corrective strategies before monthly close. BCP benefited from improved SLA performance and service visibility, while the governance model and tool remained internal to TCS.
